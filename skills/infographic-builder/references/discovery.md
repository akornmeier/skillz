# Discovery and briefing

Use this reference when the request is broad, the inputs are mixed, or the output channel is unclear.

## Triage the request

Classify what the user supplied:

- **Topic only:** research scope and sources are missing.
- **Narrative prose:** claims must be extracted and prioritized.
- **Dataset:** fields, definitions, units, nulls, and methods need inspection.
- **Report or deck:** find the decision, not merely the table of contents.
- **Existing infographic:** identify whether the task is critique, correction, redesign, or adaptation.
- **Image prompt:** distinguish concept generation from factual production.

Classify the requested result:

- content brief;
- evidence/story recommendation;
- wireframe or storyboard;
- production prompt;
- editable SVG/HTML/component;
- final raster/print export;
- critique and repair.

## Ask high-value questions only

Ask questions whose answers materially change accuracy or direction. Prefer a compact batch:

1. Who is the primary audience, and what should they do or remember?
2. Which claims/data are mandatory, and which sources are authoritative?
3. Where will this appear, at what dimensions or aspect ratio?
4. Is the deliverable a plan, editable source, final export, or generator prompt?
5. What brand, tone, language, accessibility, or confidentiality rules apply?
6. May third-party services receive any supplied material?

Do not ask users to choose a chart template before understanding the story.

## Sensible assumptions

When the user is unavailable, state assumptions in the brief. Safe defaults:

- one general audience;
- one main takeaway;
- static, accessible SVG or self-contained HTML;
- vertical reading order for reports/social and landscape for presentations;
- system fonts and restrained palette;
- deterministic charts and text;
- no external upload or paid service.

These are defaults, not facts. Do not infer a target audience, publication deadline, or source authority without labeling the assumption.

## Attention budget

Match content density to viewing context:

- **Social/mobile:** one claim, 1–3 supporting facts, short labels.
- **Presentation:** one idea per slide/visual; readable at distance.
- **Report/editorial:** richer annotation and methods, but a clear first-pass story.
- **Poster/exhibit:** strong distance hierarchy and wayfinding.
- **Interactive web:** overview first, details on demand, non-interactive equivalent.

If the evidence requires more detail than the channel supports, recommend a series, carousel, multi-panel page, or companion source table rather than shrinking everything.
