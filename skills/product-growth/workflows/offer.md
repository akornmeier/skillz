# Offer workflow

## Owned job

Shape what is sold and how its value is presented: audience/outcome framing, package and tier language, core deliverables, value metric, bonuses, guarantee or risk reversal, offer name, stated prices, payment terms, and offer-specific objection framing.

**Excludes:** pricing-page layout or copy-only work, paywall behavior, lead magnets, proposals and sales collateral, launch/channel plans, sales operations, and unsupported pricing or willingness-to-pay research.

## Input checklist

Confirm or derive:

- Buyer, buying stage, job/problem, desired outcome, and next-best alternative
- Business/product type and sales motion
- Core deliverable, delivery method, time to first value, and customer effort
- Existing package/tier limits, value metric, stated price, billing period, and payment terms
- Available proof, objections, implementation facts, support, and constraints
- Bonus assets, refund tolerance, measurable outcomes, and any real capacity/date/inventory limit
- Requested artifact: offer brief, package recommendation, value/price presentation, or objection response

## Process

1. **Restate the current offer.** Write one plain sentence covering buyer, outcome, deliverable, price if stated, and purchase terms. Mark unknowns.
2. **Diagnose value.** Evaluate outcome desirability, perceived likelihood, time to value, and customer effort. Pick the weakest supported lever; do not default to discounting.
3. **Design the package.** Define core deliverables, boundaries, value metric, tier or payment distinctions, implementation path, and who each option is for. Keep choices meaningfully different.
4. **Add only useful extras.** A bonus must remove a real blocker or accelerate the core outcome.
5. **Reverse the actual risk.** Match guarantee terms to buyer risk, outcome measurability, delivery model, and refund tolerance. State eligibility, period, remedy, and at most two necessary conditions. Prefer no guarantee over an unsustainable promise.
6. **Apply shared integrity thresholds.** Use `../references/voice-and-evidence.md` to decide whether proof, comparisons, guarantee wording, or urgency may appear and how strongly it may be stated.
7. **Draft value language.** Lead with buyer outcome, explain mechanism and package, connect features to business value, show the user's stated price plainly, address the strongest objection, then give one next action.
8. **Expose decisions.** Separate ready-to-ship language, assumptions, evidence gaps, and highest-priority validation step.

## Stop conditions

- Buyer, desired outcome, or core deliverable is unknown: ask one compact question before drafting.
- User asks what price the market will bear without research: provide a research plan or provisional test framing, not a number presented as fact.
- Guarantee, urgency, proof, or comparison wording fails the loaded integrity rules: apply that reference's safe treatment and continue.

## Source note

The four-lever value diagnosis adapts Alex Hormozi's Value Equation from *$100M Offers*.

## Route quality check

- Can a buyer tell in one scan who it is for, desired outcome, what is included, stated price/terms, risk allocation, and next step?
- Does every bonus strengthen the core outcome rather than inflate a value stack?
- Are package distinctions tied to buyer value and a coherent value metric?
- Is every guarantee sustainable and every urgency claim true, specific, and enforceable?
- Are stated prices preserved without implying unsupported pricing validation?
